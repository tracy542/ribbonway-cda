Lead-Based Paint Disclosure

West Virginia · EPBR Lead Paint 8/2017 · 1 page · Any home built before 1978

Built before 1978? Seller completes and signs at listing, buyer initials every acknowledgment and gets the EPA pamphlet, and both agents sign. Every initial line and every "(i) or (ii)" choice gets filled.

Open the blank form Open the EPA pamphlet

Purpose of this form

  • The federal lead-based paint disclosure for the sale of a home built before 1978.
  • The seller says what they know about lead paint and hands over any reports. The buyer acknowledges the warning, the reports, the pamphlet, and the inspection opportunity.
  • Both agents acknowledge their duty to make sure it's done.

Before you start

  • Check the year built on the tax record and the MLS. If they disagree, sort it out before listing.
  • Listing side: collect any lead-paint inspection or risk-assessment reports the seller has. They get listed on the form and go to the buyer.
  • Buyer side: get the seller-completed form from the listing agent, and have the EPA pamphlet ready to give the buyer.

Fill it in, top to bottom

Top — property and age (Section I)

  • For the sale of Property at — the full address.
  • Each seller initials one of the three lines and states the Year Constructed:
    • Built after January 1, 1978 (all portions) — form says complete Section V only.
    • Built before January 1, 1978 (any portion) — complete all sections.
    • Seller can't say how old it is — complete all sections.

Section II — Seller disclosure

  • Each seller completes (a) and (b).
  • (a) Check (i) known lead paint / hazards are present (explain) or (ii) no knowledge.
  • (b) Check (i) seller has provided all records and reports (list them) or (ii) seller has none.

Section III — Purchaser acknowledgment

  • Each purchaser initials c, d, e and f.
  • c — read the Lead Warning Statement.
  • d — received copies of the reports listed in II(b). If none were listed, initial here anyway.
  • e — received the pamphlet Protect Your Family from Lead in Your Home.
  • f — initial (i) received a 10-day (or agreed) opportunity for a risk assessment or inspection, or (ii) waived it. This should match what the contract and addenda say about a lead inspection.

Section IV — Agent acknowledgment

  • Initial g. Listing and selling agents both.

Section V — Certification and signatures

  • Every seller, every purchaser, and both agents sign and date.

Blue Ribbon rules

  • Required when the home was built before 1978 (federal law).
  • Seller, buyer and both agents sign.
  • The buyer gets the EPA pamphlet.
  • Listing side: the seller section is completed and signed at listing.
  • Signing order: NOA → MLS sheet → dual agency (if ours) → HUD home inspection notice (FHA) → seller's disclosure → lead paint → Residential Sales Contract → WV ABA → Items to Convey → one financing addendum → other addenda → pre-approval or proof of funds.
  • Every blank filled in or marked N/A.
  • Final fully signed copy goes in the deal's WAFFL folder in zipForm/dotloop.

Common mistakes

  • Year Constructed left blank, or more than one age line initialed.
  • Only one seller (or one buyer) initialed. Each one initials.
  • Neither (i) nor (ii) checked in II(a) or II(b).
  • Reports checked as provided in II(b)(i) but not listed, or never given to the buyer.
  • Buyer skipped "d" because no reports were listed.
  • Neither f(i) nor f(ii) initialed.
  • Pamphlet not given, but "e" initialed anyway.
  • One of the agents didn't initial "g" or sign Section V.
